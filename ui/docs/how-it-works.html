How a parcel travels

Follow one parcel from the app that sends it to the laptop that does the work, including the two detours: waiting for you, and waiting for the laptop.

The app drops a parcel at the Hub with its key. The Hub checks the contract and answers at once. If the contract says so, the parcel waits for you. Then the Hub hands it to the laptop, which checks again, does the work and sends the result back. The app can ask for the result at any time. If the laptop is away, the parcel simply waits its turn.

The whole journey

The steps in plain words

  1. The app sends the parcel. It posts to the Hub with its key in the Authorization header. A revoked key, or a key whose contract has expired, is turned away at the door with a 401. Nothing is recorded as a parcel.
  2. The Hub checks the contract. This is check 1. It looks at the expiry, the task, the daily limit and the path. The rules are in Keys and contracts.
  3. The Hub answers at once. Three outcomes:
    • queued The parcel is allowed. The app gets a 202.
    • waiting_approval The parcel is allowed but needs you. The app also gets a 202.
    • refused The parcel broke a rule. The app gets a 403 with the reason. The parcel is still written down, but it never reaches the laptop.
  4. The Hub tells you. A waiting parcel shows under "Needs you" on the Overview page and on the Approvals page. The browser extension also shows a badge (view only: you approve in the app).
  5. You decide. Approve moves it to queued. Decline sets it to refused with the reason "declined by owner". Before it moves, the Hub checks the contract once more, so an approval cannot get past a contract you have since tightened.
  6. The Hub hands it to the laptop. The Hub sends one parcel at a time, oldest first, and only when the laptop is free. Just before sending it checks the contract again. The parcel becomes running.
  7. The laptop checks again and works. This is check 2. The laptop re-checks the contract that came with the job, using real paths, its own home folder and its own clock. Then it runs the task.
  8. The result comes back. done with a result, or failed with an error. If the laptop's own check says no, the parcel becomes refused.
  9. The app asks. The app reads GET /api/jobs/<id> and sees the state and, when done, the result. It can only see its own key's parcels.

When the laptop is away

The Hub does not need the laptop to accept a parcel. Allowed parcels stay queued. When the laptop connects, the Hub sends them in the order they arrived. The Laptop page shows them under "In the queue". If the laptop drops while a parcel is running, that parcel goes back to queued and runs again after the laptop returns.

A parcel that was running when the laptop dropped can run a second time, because the Hub cannot know how far it got. Keep tasks safe to repeat, or watch for it.

The laptop dials out

Your laptop sits behind your home or office network. It cannot be reached from outside, and you do not have to change any settings. Instead the daemon opens a WebSocket (a long-lived two-way connection) to the Hub. It sends "hello" and the last event number it saw. The Hub answers "welcome" and sends any waiting jobs.

  • The Hub pings every 25 seconds. If the laptop does not answer within 60 seconds, the Hub treats it as gone.
  • If the connection drops, the daemon tries again with a growing wait, from 1 second up to 30 seconds.
  • Only one laptop is live at a time. A new valid connection replaces the old one.
Under the hood: one writer, and an event for everything
  • One writer. The Hub is a single Durable Object (one small program with its own SQLite database, hosted by Cloudflare). Every request goes through it, one at a time, so two parcels can never corrupt each other and the daily limit is counted in one place.
  • Events. Every change appends a line to an append-only log with a rising number (seq): job.queued, job.approved, job.running, job.done and so on. A parcel's "Tracking" list is built from these. See Events.
  • Live stream. The Events page listens to /admin/events/stream, a Server-Sent Events stream. It first replays what you missed after a given number, then sends new events as they happen, with a small ping every 20 seconds.
  • Same rule, two places. The Hub and the daemon both import shared/contract.ts.

Open a finished parcel on the Parcels page and read "Tracking". Each line is one event from this journey. Then open the Events page and find the same lines.
