The big picture

The Mailroom is a small office for your software: it takes requests at a front desk, checks them against rules, and hands the safe ones to your laptop.

An app sends a parcel (a request) with a key (who is sending). The Hub (the front desk, in the cloud) checks it against the key's contract (what that sender may do). Safe parcels go to your laptop, risky ones wait for you, and anything outside the rules is refused. The laptop checks again before it does anything.

The mailroom idea

Think of the mail room in a company. Anyone can drop a parcel at the front desk. The clerk does not just pass it on. The clerk looks at who sent it and reads the rules for that sender. Then the clerk does one of three things.

  • Allow it. The parcel goes to the person who does the work.
  • Hold it. The parcel waits on a shelf until you say yes.
  • Turn it away. The clerk writes down why. Nothing is delivered.

The Mailroom works the same way. The work is done by Claude Code and a few small tasks on one laptop: yours.

Two checks, and why

Every parcel is checked twice with the same rule, which lives in one file (shared/contract.ts).

  1. The gateway check, in the cloud. It runs when the parcel arrives. A parcel that breaks the contract is refused here and never leaves the Hub.
  2. The laptop check, on your machine. It runs again just before the work starts, using the contract that arrives with the job. It also looks at real paths, so a shortcut (a symlink) that points outside an allowed folder is caught. It trusts the Hub for what the contract says.

Why twice? The cloud is someone else's computer. The laptop sees things the cloud cannot, such as where a symlink really points, and it catches Hub bugs and stale state. With a laptop policy file (mailroom policy) it also guards against a compromised Hub that sends a wider contract; without one it does not.

The contract limits where a task may work, not what Claude does there. A claude task runs claude -p with whatever permissions that command has on your laptop. Keep claude out of contracts you do not trust.

What it is not

  • Not a public service. There is one owner and one laptop. There are no user accounts and no sign-up.
  • Not for other people's machines. It is built for personal use on your own computer.
  • Not a way into your laptop. Nothing from the internet connects to it. The laptop connects out (see How a parcel travels).
  • Not a way around your approval. A task the contract marks "needs approval" does not run until you click.
